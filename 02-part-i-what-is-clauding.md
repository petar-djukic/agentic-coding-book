# Part I — The Skill

Agent-based development is a distinct skill from programming, and the
distinction is not about prompts. It is about which decisions get
written down, in what form, and what verifies the result.

This part establishes the vocabulary the rest of the book uses. Chapter
1 separates the two kinds of intent an agent needs and introduces the
six levels of autonomy. Chapter 2 covers how construction is ordered —
the inner and outer loops, and why agents automate only one of them.
Chapter 3 explains what the model is actually doing, because the mental
model determines what a programmer does with the output. Chapter 4
applies all of it to a decision every project starts with: which
language.
